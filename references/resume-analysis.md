# Resume analysis

Analyze the supplied resume as a source of candidate facts and as the formatting baseline. Do not infer a fact merely because it would be typical for the role.

## Extract candidate evidence

Capture only details relevant to the target role:

- Role titles, employers, dates, and career chronology.
- Responsibilities, projects, actions, and the user's level of ownership.
- Technologies, tools, methods, and domain experience explicitly stated.
- Outcomes, scale, and metrics, preserving units and context.
- Education, certifications, licenses, and any stated status or dates.
- Skills and other relevant sections.

Keep distinctions such as team versus individual work, leadership versus contribution, and implementation versus exposure. Do not infer proficiency, recency, or years of experience from a keyword alone. Preserve the wording of precise claims in your working notes so later edits can be checked against the source.

## Record the formatting baseline

When the document is accessible, note its file type, page count, section order, heading hierarchy, approximate typography, margins, columns, bullet styles, and header/footer elements. Treat these observations as constraints for the tailored version.

PDF text extraction can scramble columns, omit information in graphics, or lose visual hierarchy. Cross-check extracted text against the rendered pages when tools support it. If content or structure remains uncertain, ask the user or leave it unchanged instead of guessing.

## Prioritize, do not rewrite indiscriminately

Select existing content that is most relevant to the target role. Improve clarity and ordering without changing the underlying claim. Add portfolio-supported facts only after validating their source and attribution. Do not replace every bullet, remove unrelated but important context, or change dates and titles simply to match the job posting.

## Questions

Ask a focused question only when a missing fact could materially strengthen or change a proposed claim, such as personal contribution, scale, or an outcome. Do not demand answers for every missing metric. Provide a useful, conservative draft when possible.
